# 🎯 Problem Statement

## Background

The power distribution industry depends on transformers, substations, and other grid equipment to deliver reliable electricity to homes, businesses, and critical facilities. Equipment failures can cause unexpected power outages, service disruptions, costly repairs, and significant economic losses.

Traditional maintenance practices often rely on **fixed schedules and periodic inspections**. However, grid equipment can develop failure conditions between scheduled inspections. Factors such as **high electrical load, abnormal temperature, equipment age, previous failures, maintenance history, weather conditions, and outage history** can increase the likelihood of equipment failure and power outages.

With increasing availability of equipment health and environmental data, utilities have an opportunity to move from reactive maintenance toward **risk-based and predictive maintenance**.

## The Problem

Utilities need a way to combine **equipment health indicators, operational conditions, weather factors, and historical failure/outage information** to identify which grid assets are most likely to fail and which potential failures could cause the greatest impact.

The key challenges are:

- Identifying equipment with a high probability of failure.
- Detecting areas with elevated outage risk.
- Combining multiple risk factors instead of evaluating them separately.
- Prioritizing the most vulnerable assets for maintenance.
- Understanding the potential impact of an equipment failure.
- Providing clear and actionable recommendations to grid operators.

Without a centralized risk-based approach, operators may have difficulty determining **which asset requires attention first and where maintenance resources should be prioritized**.

## Who is Affected

The primary users are **utility grid operators, maintenance teams, asset managers, and field crews** responsible for monitoring and maintaining transformers, substations, and other critical electrical infrastructure.

These teams need to make timely decisions about:

- Which equipment should be inspected first.
- Which assets require preventive maintenance.
- Which grid zones have elevated outage risk.
- Which failures could affect the largest number of customers or critical facilities.
- How maintenance resources should be prioritized.

Ultimately, electricity customers, businesses, and critical facilities are also affected when equipment failures result in unexpected outages.

## Why It Matters

Unexpected grid equipment failures can result in **power outages, equipment damage, emergency maintenance costs, customer disruption, and economic losses**.

The impact can be particularly severe when a highly critical asset fails, because a single equipment failure may affect a large number of customers or important facilities.

Moving from purely schedule-based maintenance toward **risk-based prioritization** can help utilities:

- Identify high-risk assets earlier.
- Prioritize preventive maintenance.
- Reduce the likelihood of unexpected failures.
- Improve grid reliability and operational planning.
- Better allocate limited maintenance and field resources.
- Reduce the potential impact of equipment-related outages.

## Why Existing Solutions Fall Short

Traditional approaches commonly depend on **scheduled maintenance, periodic inspections, individual sensor readings, and historical records**. While these methods provide valuable information, they may not provide a unified view of the overall risk of each asset.
